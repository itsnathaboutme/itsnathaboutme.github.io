How I built a Budget and Training Tracker

One source of truth for Live Training schedules, budgets, and task lists. It became the standard tool for the whole operations team.

View sample

Part 1: The Budget and Training Tracker

The problem

When I joined the Live Training operations team, there was no centralized system to track training schedules, instructor assignments, and budget allocation in one place. Teams were working off separate spreadsheets that were often outdated, which led to:

  • Miscommunication between project managers and instructors
  • Budget overruns going undetected until it was too late
  • Scheduling conflicts and missed slots during peak training periods
  • No visibility into overall departmental spending at a glance

What I did

I built a consolidated Budget and Training Tracker from scratch using Google Sheets. The tracker was designed to be the single source of truth for all Live Training operations. The sample linked above is a recreated version with demonstration data.

Key features I built into the tracker:

  • Training Schedule tab: full schedule with dates, instructors, client and venues, and attendance
  • Dashboard tab: summary cards showing total budget, spent, and remaining at a glance
  • Budget Tracker tab: line-item budget per training program with auto-calculated variances and trainer receipts
  • Task list: color-coded indicators for Completed, In Progress, Scheduled, and Cancelled
  • Auto-totals: formulas that update in real time as data is entered

The format was set up as a general template, which can be expanded as needed.

Key formulas

Four formulas do most of the work. Each one is a formula from the sample workbook.

Variance (Budget Tracker, one row per program)
=F6-G6

F6 is the budget and G6 is the amount spent. A positive number means the program is under budget.

Percent used (Budget Tracker summary row)
=IFERROR(F3/E3,0)

Total spent divided by total budget. IFERROR shows 0% instead of an error while the budget is still blank.

Count programs by status (summary row)
=COUNTIF(E6:E25,"Completed")

Counts rows whose status is "Completed". The same pattern works for "In Progress", "Scheduled", and "Cancelled".

Cross-sheet reference (Dashboard)
='Budget Tracker'!E3

The Dashboard reads its totals from the Budget Tracker, so each total is entered once and reused everywhere.

Color-coded status

Each status has a fixed color, set with conditional formatting, so rows change color on their own when the status changes. A Legend sheet explains the colors.

The result

Within the first quarter of rollout, the tracker was adopted as the standard tool across the entire team. Key outcomes included:

  • Significant improvement in departmental efficiency, with no more chasing updates across multiple files
  • Full budget visibility for all Live Training programs in one view
  • Zero critical backlog maintained across all tracked initiatives
  • Project managers were able to make faster, more informed decisions
  • The tool was eventually endorsed and used by the wider operations team

Tools used

  • Google Sheets
  • Formulas: SUMIFS, COUNTIF, IFERROR, and dynamic cross-sheet references
  • Conditional formatting for status colors, and dropdowns
  • Shared with stakeholders through Google Drive for real-time collaboration

This case study shows my ability to identify an operational gap, design a practical solution from the ground up, and drive adoption across a team without formal technical training. It reflects my approach to every project: find the problem, build something that works, and make sure it actually gets used.

Part 2: The End-to-End Training Task List

Even with a Budget and Training Tracker in place, tasks were still falling through the cracks on the ground. There was no structured, end-to-end checklist covering what needed to happen before, during, and after each training. This led to:

  • Last-minute scrambles to confirm venues, materials, and instructor availability
  • Inconsistent handoffs between pre-training preparation and delivery
  • Attendee feedback forms being missed or collected too late
  • Post-training wrap-up tasks (reports, budget updates, archiving) being delayed or forgotten
  • No single reference point for the team to align on who owns what and by when

What I did

I built a structured task list template for each Live Training program, designed to run alongside the Budget and Training Tracker as the operational execution layer. It covers every phase of a training, from weeks out to days after completion.

  • Phase-based structure: tasks grouped into Pre-Training, During Training, and Post-Training
  • 27 tasks per training: covering logistics, communications, delivery, and wrap-up
  • Owner column: assigned to the Project Assistant, the Instructor, or shared
  • Due date column: relative deadlines such as "3 weeks before", "Day of training", and "2 days after"
  • Priority: High, Medium, or Low, for quick triage
  • Status tracking: Done, In Progress, or Scheduled for each task
  • Checkbox column: a simple visual progress indicator
  • Color-coded phase headers: instant visual separation between phases

The result

The task list became the go-to execution companion for every training program. Key outcomes included:

  • No more missed tasks, since every stakeholder knew what was due and when
  • Smoother handoffs between pre-training preparation and live delivery
  • Consistent post-training wrap-up, with reports, budget updates, and archiving completed on time
  • Fewer back-and-forth messages with project managers, because the task list answered most status questions upfront
  • Paired with the Budget Tracker, it gave full operational visibility from planning to closeout

Tools used

  • Google Sheets, with Microsoft Excel for the demo version
  • Conditional formatting for phase headers, priority badges, and status colors
  • Shared through Google Drive, so all team members could access it in real time
  • Used alongside Jira and Monday.com for project-level tracking

Combined with the Budget Tracker, it reflects how I approach operations: not just keeping things on track, but building the systems that make it easier for everyone to stay on track.
